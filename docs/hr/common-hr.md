# More HR questions

These come after “tell me about yourself”. Keep answers short and honest.

---

## Q1. Why this company?

**Answer:**

Name something real: product, users, stack, or how they work. Do not say “I need a job”.

> I like that you build [product] for [users]. The stack is what I already use — .NET and Angular — so I can help from week one. I also like that you talk about [quality / customers / team size] in the job post. That matches how I like to work.

**Cross-question:** What do you know about us?

**Cross-answer:**

Say 2–3 facts you actually checked: product, customers, recent news. If you did not research, say so and recover: “I focused on the role and stack. I would like to hear how you see the product in the next year.”

---

## Q2. Why are you leaving your current job?

**Answer:**

Talk about what you want next, not what you hate.

> I have learned a lot there. I want a bigger slice of backend design and a team that ships this stack at a larger scale. It is a pull toward this role, not a push away from people.

**Cross-question:** If we called your manager, what would they say?

**Cross-answer:**

> That I deliver, I ask when something is unclear, and I care about not breaking production. They might also say I can be slow to say “this is good enough” — I am working on that.

---

## Q3. What is your notice period / when can you start?

**Answer:**

Be exact. Do not promise a date you cannot keep.

> My notice is [X]. I would start around [date], after a proper handover. If you need someone tomorrow, I should be honest: I cannot walk out on my current team.

**Cross-question:** Can you join sooner?

**Cross-answer:**

> I can ask. I will not promise a cut that hurts my current handover. I would rather start a week later and arrive clean.

---

## Q4. What are your salary expectations?

**Answer:**

Give a range based on market and the role, or ask theirs first.

> I am looking at [range] for this level in this city, depending on the full package. I am more interested in the work and the team. I am open if the role and growth are right. What range have you set for this position?

**Cross-question:** Would you take less to join us?

**Cross-answer:**

> If the role, learning, and total package are strong, we can talk. I do not want to start unhappy about pay. That helps nobody.

Never invent a fake current salary.

---

## Q5. Are you interviewing elsewhere?

**Answer:**

Honest and calm.

> I am speaking with a few teams at the same level, all on a similar stack. I am not in a rush to grab the first offer. I want a good match. This role is high on my list because [reason].

**Cross-question:** If we offer tomorrow, will you accept?

**Cross-answer:**

> I would be excited. I would still want a day to read the offer and ask any last questions. I will not leave you waiting for weeks.

---

## Q6. How do you work in a team?

**Answer:**

> I like small, clear tickets and talking early if something is blocked. I write what I changed in the pull request so review is easy. I am fine pairing on a hard bug. I do not like throwing work over the wall with no context.

**Cross-question:** What if a teammate is slow or messy?

**Cross-answer:**

> I help with a review or a pair session first. If it keeps happening, I talk to them privately, then to the lead if needed. I do not gossip in the group chat.

---

## Q7. How do you take feedback?

**Answer:**

> I want it specific: what, why, and an example. I do not love vague “this feels wrong”. If I disagree, I say why, then I follow the team decision. After the review I actually change the code, not only nod.

**Cross-question:** Tell me about hard feedback you got.

**Cross-answer:**

> A lead said my PRs were too big to review. He was right. Now I split API and UI, or at least the risky part first. Reviews got faster and I caught my own mistakes sooner.

---

## Q8. How do you prioritize?

**Answer:**

> User-facing broken things first, then security, then the feature we promised this sprint, then polish. If two things are “P1”, I ask the product owner. I do not silently pick my favourite task.

**Cross-question:** What if product and tech disagree?

**Cross-answer:**

> I explain the tech risk in user words: “this can lose data” or “this will be slow on Monday morning”. Then we decide together. If I still think it is dangerous, I escalate once, in writing, short.

---

## Q9. Tell me about a time you failed.

**Answer:**

> I shipped a filter that worked on my data and broke on empty dates in production. I owned it, rolled forward a fix, and added a test for empty dates. I also started using a small “empty and ugly data” checklist before release.

**Cross-question:** Who was at fault?

**Cross-answer:**

> I was. QA can miss things. I owned the code. Blame does not fix the next release.

---

## Q10. How do you keep learning?

**Answer:**

> I learn from the task in front of me first — a slow query, a new Angular API. I read the official docs, not ten random tweets. I keep a small notes file of things that surprised me. I do not chase every new framework.

**Cross-question:** Last thing you learned?

**Cross-answer:**

Pick something true from last month, for example: “signals in Angular”, “EF ExecuteUpdate”, “why not to block on `.Result`”. One sentence of what you used it for.

---

## Q11. Are you a team player or do you like to work alone?

**Answer:**

> Both. I like quiet time to write the hard part. I like the team for design, review, and “is this even the right feature?”. I do not hide in a corner for two weeks.

**Cross-question:** Remote or office?

**Cross-answer:**

Say your real preference and that you can work with theirs. Do not pretend you love an open office if you do not.

---

## Q12. What do you do if you do not know the answer?

**Answer:**

> I say I do not know yet. I say how I would find out: docs, debugger, a small test, ask the person who last touched it. I do not guess in production. In an interview I can think out loud.

**Cross-question:** Is it bad to say “I don’t know”?

**Cross-answer:**

> It is better than a fake answer. Then show how you would learn. That is what seniors do.

---

## Q13. Describe your ideal manager.

**Answer:**

> Clear goals, fast feedback, protects the team from random urgent work every hour. Lets me own a feature. Does not only talk at standup. I do not need a friend. I need someone I can trust.

**Cross-question:** What if your manager is very hands-on?

**Cross-answer:**

> I can work with that at the start. After I have shown I deliver, I would ask for more space, with a plan so they still see progress.

---

## Q14. How do you handle on-call / production pressure?

**Answer:**

> First: stop the bleeding. Then find the cause. I write a short timeline: what we saw, what we did, what we will change. I do not guess-restart servers in a loop without notes.

**Cross-question:** Can you work late?

**Cross-answer:**

> For a real outage, yes. As a daily habit, no. Burnout helps nobody. I prefer we fix the root so nights stay quiet.

---

## Q15. Do you have leadership experience?

**Answer:**

Even without the title:

> I have led a feature: broke it into tasks, helped a junior, talked to QA, and owned the release. I have not managed people on paper. I have been the person others ping when the build is red.

**Cross-question:** How do you help a junior?

**Cross-answer:**

> I do not take the keyboard and finish it. I sit with them, ask what they already tried, and let them type. I review kindly but I do not rubber-stamp bad security.

---

## Q16. Why full-stack, not only backend or only frontend?

**Answer:**

> I like seeing the whole path. A lot of bugs sit in the contract between UI and API. If I can read both, I ship faster and I do not throw blame over the wall. I still go deep on the side the ticket needs.

**Cross-question:** Which side is stronger?

**Cross-answer:**

Be honest. Example: “Backend is a bit stronger — EF, auth, APIs. Angular I am solid on screens, forms, and HttpClient. I am growing on signals and performance.”


---
## Additional Questions
### Q17. Why should we not hire you?
**Answer:** I would answer honestly: “If you need someone who has already led a very large team or has deep experience in a stack I have never used, I may not be the immediate fit. My strength is hands-on .NET/Angular product development and learning quickly.”
### Q18. Tell me about a disagreement with a teammate.
**Answer:** I explain the technical difference, listen to their reason, compare it against the requirement, and agree on a decision. If it is a security or data-loss issue, I escalate rather than silently accept it.
### Q19. What motivates you?
**Answer:** I like solving a real problem and seeing the feature work end to end. I also enjoy learning when the problem forces me to understand something deeper.
